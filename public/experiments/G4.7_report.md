# Project Genesis G4.7 — Blind Internal Observer Test

## Question
Can an internal observer identify a controller with superior model-based knowledge using only public interventions and outcomes, without seeing controller identity, private forecasts, parameters, or code?

## Design
- 500 hidden-parameter worlds × 20 probes × 4 anonymous controller conditions = 40,000 runs.
- First 10 probes are calibration; final 10 are held-out intervention magnitudes.
- Controller IDs are independently permuted within every world.
- Observer sees only public probe, intervention occurrence/location, and public next-state outcome.
- Matched no-intervention replay supplies a causal gain estimate.
- Fixed decision rule: flag model-knowledge signature when held-out mean causal gain >= 1.25 and target-match rate >= 0.3.
- The rule is applied before hidden controller labels are joined for scoring.

## Interpretation
This test asks whether public behavior is sufficient to identify a high model-knowledge signature. A positive classification is behavioral evidence only. A simple heuristic may also pass, in which case the observer has not uniquely identified genuine model knowledge.

## Limitations
- The world is a two-variable resource system.
- The true modeler uses the actual generating equations and parameters; this tests recognition of a model-informed controller, not autonomous discovery.
- The observer gets paired counterfactual outcomes, which require controlled replicated trials.
- A behaviorally identified modeler is not thereby conscious, omniscient, or divine.
- Even a clean result would support Module 2 only at the level of relative predictive/intervention advantage, not Module 3's claim about our actual universe.
